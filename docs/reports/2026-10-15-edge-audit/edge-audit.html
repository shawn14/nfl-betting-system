Why the prediction record fails the forward-test standard

The NFL job refreshes team scoring averages from current ESPN standings, selects completed games, and then calculates the prediction it grades. The version label is attached at that point; it does not mean the prediction was locked before kickoff. The published forecast also applies weather and quarterback adjustments that the grader omits. The same postgame recalculation pattern exists in the NHL and WNBA grading paths.

Verified in stat refresh, completed-game selection, prediction generation, version stamping and published forecast adjustments. A separate reviewer fetched the live data and reproduced the finding.

Sixteen entries call the initial observation their “close”; all sixteen are positive. Nineteen closing observations are more than an hour before kickoff, with one 56 hours early. Removing these is a measurement-quality check, not a strategy optimization. The positive average then no longer clearly differs from zero.

The separate props replay reports +0.60 pp across 863 flags, but those observations come from only 47 games and repeat across snapshots and books. Its simple standard error treats them as independent, so the advertised precision is overstated. This audit inspected that replay methodology; it did not rerun all 3,121 archived prop snapshots.

What would establish an edge

  1. Record the exact published prediction before kickoff. Save its timestamp, model version, input cutoff, book, price and line once. Grade only that immutable record; never regenerate a prediction after the result.
  2. Keep rules fixed and test forward. Evaluate NFL totals and price shopping separately against the available market, with fees and actual execution where applicable. Report returns, calibration and closing value, not just wins.
  3. Require a genuinely later, timely closing reference. Exclude missing/stale closes explicitly, group uncertainty by game, and keep all selected bets in the denominator. A 50–100 bet check can detect failures; it is not enough to reliably establish a small edge.

Additional verified data issue: NFL scoring averages divide by wins + losses, omitting ties. Current NYG and WSH data therefore divide five games by four. That inflates averages and should be fixed before prospective evaluation. These are findings and recommended next steps, not implemented changes.
